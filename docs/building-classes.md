# Building classes: what the city's footprints are

Every building in the detailed city that `?massing=1` stands on its City 3D Massing outline (35,254 records), measured
and sorted into classes, so the prototype list in the building-prototypes spec (`st-ndw9`, phase 0a, §4) can be drawn
from counts rather than guesswork. Bead `st-ndw9.1`. The spec itself is not in this repo.

Generated by `node tools/report-building-classes.mjs --md docs/building-classes.md`. Every number here comes from its
output, `tools/building-classes.json`. Nothing the page loads was changed.

## Method

### Inputs

- **`js/toronto-geodata.js`** `GEO.buildings`: one row per building record, `[cx, cz, angle, boxes, height, kind, roof,
  colour seed, 0, name]` (column meanings in `tools/geo-features.mjs` and `js/buildings.js`). Height is the lidar
  maximum, or for a pitched-roof house the average (nearer the eaves). Kind: 0 house, 1 low-rise, 2 mid-rise, 3 tower,
  4 shed, 5 works, 6 worship, 7 civic.
- **`data/toronto-massing.bin`**: each record's outer ring and courtyards, party-wall edges, hidden edges, street
  edges (and whether on a main street) and its base, read through `js/massing-data.js` `massingRows` exactly as
  `tools/report-massing.mjs` does.

### Per record

- **Area**: the outer ring's area less each courtyard's (the ring's shoelace, courtyards negative).
- **Perimeter**: the outer ring's edge lengths.
- **Oriented width and depth, and the shorter side**: the minimum-area rectangle around the outer ring (rotating calipers
  over its edges); its long side (width), short side (depth), and the shorter of the two.
- **Height and storeys**: the row's height; storeys = height / 3.4 m (the houses' storey, `js/buildings.js`
  `FLOOR_HEIGHTS[0]`).
- **Kind and roof code**: straight from the row.
- **Party walls**: the count of party edges. Party edges are grouped into facing sides (below).
- **Neighbours and joined groups**: the sidecar stores which edge is a party edge but not which building is behind it.
  Each party edge's three probes (0.15, 0.5, 0.85 along it, 0.35 m outward: `tools/build-massing.mjs`
  `PARTY_PROBE`) are tested against every other record's outer ring; the record that holds the most probes is the
  neighbour. The party-wall graph's connected components are the joined groups (how many records are chained together).
- **Street edges**: count, total frontage length, and whether any edge is on a main street (9 m or wider).
- **Podium and courtyard**: base above 0; more than one ring.
- **Latitude and longitude**: js/city-config.js latLonFromWorld (inverse of worldFromLatLon; the same as tools/geo-base.mjs toLonLat). The frame origin (0, 0) comes out at
  43.6453, -79.3806 (the frame's lat0, lon0: checked).

### Attachment: party sides and joined groups

The rules are the table at the top of `tools/report-building-classes.mjs`. In short:

1. Take party edges 1.5 m or longer.
2. Cluster them into **sides** by outward normal: walk the ring's party edges in order and add each to the side whose
   previous edge its own normal is within 0.8 (under about 37°) of; a side's normal is its edges'
   length-weighted mean.
3. Keep sides whose edges total 3 m or more.
4. **Side style** by the kept sides: **none** (none), **one** (exactly one), **both** (two or more with some pair
   opposite, dot under -0.7), **corner** (two or more, none opposite).
5. **Group style**, adding the joined group: none → **detached**; one in a group of exactly two → **pair**; other one →
   **row end**; both → **row middle**; corner → **corner**. A semi's shared wall is one whole side of each half, so a
   lone side in a group of two is the pair definition.

### Classes (the table at the top of the script)

A record class is `sideStyle/heightBand/mainStreet/kind/outbuilding`, e.g. `one/h2/side/house/in`. A group class is the
same with the group style in place of the side style, e.g. `pair/h2/side/house/in`.

- **Height band** (storeys): h1 = under 1.5, h2 = 1.5–2, h3 = 2.5–3, h4 = 3–4, h5 = 5–12, h6 = 13+.
- **Main street**: any street edge on a main street.
- **Kind**: the row's kind (0–7 above).
- **Outbuilding**: no street edge, area ≤ 60 m² and height < 5 m (the
  thresholds from the histograms below; the sensitivity table is further down).

## Limits

- **Attachment is geometric, not legal.** A party wall is an edge with a neighbour's mass against it. "Both" means two
  opposite party sides: a middle-of-row unit, or (for towers) one record of a building the City split into parts. It is
  not a survey of lot lines or ownership.
- **The side thresholds are a first cut.** 1.5 m, 0.8, 3 m and
  -0.7 are stated so they can be changed. This run reproduces the coordinator's `none` total exactly
  (21,289) and his house `none` total exactly (12,168); `one`, `both` and `corner` differ by the amounts in the Checks
  table, because the coordinator's clustering is a different mechanical choice (its exact clustering rule and how it
  merges a side are not written down in the fix note). A different cut moves records between adjacent styles (a sliver
  dropped or kept changes none to one, or one to both); the 1.5 m edge cut is what matches his `none`.
- **Records are parts.** For mid-rise, tower, worship and civic, the record count over-states buildings; read the group
  tables. A group's kind is its tallest record's, so a tower on a low podium is counted by the tower.
- **The neighbour search.** The party-wall neighbour is the record holding the most of the edge's probes; it does not
  exclude that record's courtyards (a probe inside a courtyard still counts). A record with party edges but no neighbour
  and another record's wall within 3 m is chained to it for the group size.
- **Height.** The row's height is the lidar maximum (the ridge for a pitched-roof house, and the published max for a
  building with a tall plant room). A storey estimate from it overstates low houses and any building with rooftop
  equipment; 3.4 m is a house storey, not an office one.
- **The shorter side** is the shorter side of the minimum-area rectangle, which for a house with a rear wing or an L
  plan is smaller than the lot width. The semi-pair section therefore over-counts narrow houses and under-counts wide
  ones; it is a histogram, not a measurement of how the City drew a semi.
- **Outbuildings.** The thresholds are a first cut from the histograms. The count moves between
  164 and
  353 across the +/- 20% table.
- **Kind is OpenStreetMap's type plus height, not a survey.** A house's kind is largely decided by its height and area,
  so kind and height band are not independent; the cross-tables say so.
- **Era is not measured.** Nothing here separates an 1890 semi from a 2015 infill; the spec's phase 0b covers that.
- **The bigger map (`?map=eglinton`) is not measured here.** It holds 140,541 buildings in a separate, larger geodata
  file (`data/frames/eglinton/toronto-geodata.bin`) and no massing sidecar is committed for it, so this script (which reads
  `data/toronto-massing.bin`) covers the detailed frame only. See the report's Eglinton answer.
