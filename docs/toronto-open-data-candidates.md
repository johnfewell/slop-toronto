# Toronto Open Data additions

Research date: 2026-10-28. Bead: `st-scq`. This is a recommendation, not an implementation plan or a claim that the entire catalogue was audited.

Implementation backlog: epic **`st-26p`**, with separate children for every candidate below. The bench task, **`st-26p.1`**, uses John's selected [Polyfork Cast-Iron Plaza Bench](https://polyfork.dev/asset/cast-iron-plaza-bench-b4096d). Beads holds current priorities, acceptance criteria and progress.

## Recommendation

Start with **benches and wayfinding pillars** for accurately placed street detail, then **CaféTO patios** for a larger visual improvement. Treat **physical sidewalk polygons** as a separate geometry project. Park water assets, washrooms and outdoor rinks are good additions after the street props.

Counts below are calculated from downloaded records against the existing rotated `GEO.frame.detail` rectangle, using the exact projection in `js/toronto-geodata.js`. They are candidate records, not a promise that every location can be rendered without placement checks. They are not citywide totals. Street furniture counts filter `STATUS = Existing`.

## What is already in the repo

Verified against `CREDITS.md`, `tools/geo-props.mjs`, `tools/geo-trees.mjs`, `tools/geo-blocks.mjs`, `tools/geo-roads.mjs`, `tools/geo-parksigns.mjs` and the fetch/build scripts:

- City building massing and landmark meshes; actual road-surface polygons from Edge of Roadway.
- 57,120 City street-tree records in the detailed city, rendered with genus/diameter-based model and height choices.
- 4,187 hydrants, 11,574 bike rings, 561 shelters and 2,277 litter bins from City data.
- Cycling infrastructure, traffic signals/crossovers/beacons, pedestrian crossings and measured traffic volumes.
- TTC GTFS and live surface-vehicle feeds, plus Bike Share locations and live availability.
- 347 post boxes and 240 generated park signs. Post boxes, park outlines, park paths, names and sign addresses are derived from **OpenStreetMap**, rather than a City park asset inventory. Sign locations are inferred at entrances.
- Road centrelines, lane/direction inputs and connectivity currently come from **OpenStreetMap**. We do not ingest Toronto Centreline as the main road graph.
- The sidewalk/property-line inset is a fixed **4.6 m** in `tools/geo-blocks.mjs`. Real City roadway edges improve the curb outline; this is not measured sidewalk width for every street. *(Update, `st-26p.8`: the rendered walkways now use the City's physical sidewalk polygons where mapped; the 4.6 m inset remains the fallback and the build-time property line. See [sidewalk polygons](sidewalk-polygons.md).)* Streetlights exist but their placement is procedural in `js/street-details.js`.

## Useful, but lower priority

- [Traffic Calming Database](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/traffic-calming-database/): sampled all 1,228 tabular records. It gives street sections, installation/status and counts of islands/bumps/cushions/humps, **not coordinates of each device**. The City describes a separately maintained spatial layer. Exact geometry needs that layer or additional verification before adding physical bumps and corresponding vehicle response. A street segment's count is not a set of exact placements.
- [Road Restrictions](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/road-restrictions/): a live feed could support construction barriers and closure overlays. The City cautions that applicant-submitted information is not field verified. A closure description does not supply reliable lane-by-lane barrier geometry. Begin with an optional dated overlay; routing changes would be a separate simulation feature.
- [Heritage Register](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/heritage-register/): good for discovery and prioritizing facade work. It provides address/property records, not historic facade meshes. Current package and Shapefile resource were verified, but the Shapefile was not downloaded or counted. Existing City massing already supplies much of the building volume.
- [Parks and Recreation Facilities](https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/parks-and-recreation-facilities/): sampled schema/records show park/centre locations and amenity lists, useful for names/search and matching assets. These are not outlines of each playground, basketball court or off-leash enclosure. Full table download timed out; no clipped total is claimed.

## Source quality and implementation constraints

The portal reported occasional CKAN outages during research. Package metadata and most small GeoJSON downloads succeeded after initial failures. Large pole/sidewalk downloads were not needed to establish their candidacy. Production should use cached, clipped build-time data for static scenery, not download citywide source files in the browser.

There is a meaningful inconsistency in the drinking-water package: its 4326 GeoJSON contains 1,212 records with older lowercase fields and mixes in washrooms/portable toilets, while its active datastore contains 802 water records with uppercase asset fields and dated status information. The **248 local count uses the active datastore**. A future importer should pin the chosen resource, normalize and join by asset ID, validate type and preserve source dates; it must not treat the entire static layer as fountains or assume both resources are interchangeable. The washroom GeoJSON and its datastore both had 355 records.

Many portal pages display the Open Government Licence – Toronto, while some CKAN packages have null or `notspecified` licence fields. Preserve package/resource provenance and resolve that metadata discrepancy when adding each source to `CREDITS.md`. Data records and linked artwork/images are separate inputs; linked images are not bundled models. Current City/OSM-derived combined database attribution also needs to be preserved.
