## Maker: Richter Spielgeräte GmbH + Christian Huba

- Manufacturer: **Richter Spielgeräte GmbH**, D-83112 Frasdorf, Germany.
- Designer/sculptor: **Christian Huba**, born 1968 in Zell am See (Austria); trained at the
  sculpture school in Hallein (1982–86), "Kunst und Bau" Innsbruck (1989), International
  Summer Academy Salzburg, and the Hochschule für Künste Bremen (graduated 1997 under
  Prof. Waldemar Otto). He lives and works in **Aschau im Chiemgau**, Upper Bavaria, and
  has designed larch-wood animal sculptures for Richter **since 2018**.
- Maker page: <https://www.richter-spielgeraete.de/en/our-idea/crafts-and-materials/christian-huba-441/>

### Material and construction (maker's own words)

From the maker page and the Richter catalogue *Animal sculptures made from mountain larch
trees* (05/21):

- Carved from **strong mountain larch trunks** — **not oak**. The wood is weather-hard with
  a strong, eccentric grain.
- Roughly **life-size**.
- Uncontrolled cracking is reduced by **relief cuts in the centre of the trunk** and
  **partial removal of the core (gutting)**.
- The sculptures are intended to be stroked, touched, sat on and played with; "gentle"
  quiet expressions.
- **Copyright:** the designs are the intellectual property of Richter Spielgeräte GmbH;
  Christian Huba is the licenser/idea provider. Images and sketches may not be reused
  without consent. Reference photos are therefore **private-only**; no pixels, sketches or
  3D models are shipped.

## The seven animals

The German first-hand maker interview (chiemgau24, 1 Dec 2024) names the seven Toronto
animals explicitly: **Bär, Biber, Otter, Wolf, Adler, Moschus (ein Fisch), Schildkröte** —
i.e. **bear, beaver, otter, wolf, eagle, musky (muskellunge) fish, turtle**. Each stands
for a significant clan of the region. The article also states:

- Huba worked **twelve months** on the seven sculptures (about two months on the designs).
- The **turtle** is made of **five parts** and weighs about as much as a **Fiat Panda**
  (roughly one tonne).
- The logs weighed **more than one tonne** each and needed hoists/block-and-tackle.
- The wolf is described as a **mother wolf with her cubs** (workshop photo caption).
- The client is **Toronto Waterfront**; the sculptures were shipped (truck to Hamburg,
  then container ship) for a spring park opening.
- Indigenous consultation: **Shelley Charles** (community spokesperson) guided the design;
  Huba learned symbolic constraints (e.g. an animal on its back symbolises death, on its
  belly illness). The City credit names **MCFN** and **MinoKamik Collective**.

## Captain programme-sketch and video review (2026-11-11)

The captain personally viewed the labelled Richter gallery images: they are **pencil programme sketches**, not photographs. Beaver is a rounded crouched upright form with raised head, small forepaws, two teeth and a broad flat tail. Otter is an elongated low arched form with a raised head and tapered tail. These confirm the maker’s intended vocabulary but do not individually identify the installed Toronto animals.

The public RFO video was sampled into a private 30-second contact sheet using CPU decoding. It shows the artist working on an upright painted bear and interviewing outdoors; no identifiable musky shape appeared in those sampled frames. Private references: `$WATERFRONT_RESEARCH/biidaasige/dodem-richter/video/contact.png`. The normal public video URL is `https://rfo.s3-cdn.app.welocal.cloud/media/1hyjljpxypqph/5h2gy4986h4wh/videos/173860.mp4`. No video pixels are shipped.

At public video timestamp **00:15**, the upright bear has a broad connected black eye mask, black inner ears, a very large faceted blunt black nose, and natural larch rings surrounding the pupils. At **11:45**, the view shows its huge raised forepaws against the belly and black lower-claw accents. Captain-reviewed full-resolution private stills are `video/bear-015.jpg` and `video/bear-705.jpg`. These establish the Toronto workshop finish, not an individually geolocated installed pose.
