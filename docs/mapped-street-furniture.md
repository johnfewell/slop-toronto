All three are the City's contract with Astral Out-of-Home (2007, 20 years), so they cover the coordinated street
furniture, not every bin or box in the city. The datasets give an ID, a type, the fronting street, the side and a
point. They give no heading, no size, no photograph and no surface artwork. `tools/data/poster-structures-20261126.json`
and `tools/data/publication-structures-20261126.json` keep every feature with its source block;
`tools/data/litter-receptacles-20261126.json` keeps ID, type, status, frontage, side and the point of each (the full feed
is identical to the CSV `tools/geo-props.mjs` read on 2026-10-24).

## Import: both frames

`node tools/build-open-data.mjs` projects the Existing records into each frame the page can open and writes:

- `js/toronto-street-furniture.js`: the detail map (`window.CITY.STREET_FURNITURE`);
- `js/frames/eglinton/toronto-street-furniture.js`: `?map=eglinton`, which shares the detail frame's projection and
  covers x −7900..9600, z −7300..3600. `explore.html` loads one or the other, like the sidewalks.

Each file holds the poster and publication records (`id, type, frontage, side, address, x, z`) and, for all three
datasets, the audit counts (feed, existing, removed, in frame, by type). The litter receptacles are not stored again:
`GEO.props.litterBin` in each frame's `toronto-geodata.bin` already holds them, and a test pins that count to the audit.

The pole collars (`Collar`, 6 Existing records) are a poster ring on a lamp or signal pole, not a column. None of them
lies inside either frame; the build keeps them in the data with their type and the placement rejects any type but `PPC`
(`CITY.posterStats.unsupported`).

`CITY.posterStats`, `CITY.publicationStats` and `CITY.litterStats` expose these at runtime
(`tools/shots/mapped-street-furniture.json` logs them).

## Models

Both are drawn from boxes and lathes by `node tools/build-street-furniture-models.mjs`, a script that runs once on a
developer machine and writes `assets/props/poster-column.glb` and `assets/props/publication-corral.glb`. Nothing is
downloaded, generated by a paid service or taken from a photograph, and the City gives no drawings, so **the sizes are
approximations** of the Astral units (to be corrected from a measured photograph if John shoots one).

- **Poster column**: a 16-sided drum 0.9 m across, 1.8 m of poster, a plinth, a ring and a dome, 2.54 m high, 224
  triangles in two materials. The four posters are original abstract art drawn by the same script (a dawn, a block
  composition, waves, a halftone), with no lettering, no brand and no real poster. A column is turned by its place in
  quarter turns, so each of the four faces shows to the street in turn.
- **Publication corral**: a 1.5 × 0.62 × 1.12 m open frame round three coin-operated boxes with red, blue and yellow lids
  and pale paper windows, 348 triangles, one material. Its boxes face the walkers, away from the road.
- Litter receptacles keep John's existing three-chamber bin (`assets/props/litter-bin.glb`). The City's `WR1`..`WR5`
  type codes are not described in the dataset, so one model stands for all five.
- Nothing is emissive: no real unit is known to be lit, so at night they are lit like any other prop.

## Placement

Posters and corrals use `benchSites` (`js/bench-layout.js`) with their own footprint: the named frontage first, then the
nearest road; a step back toward the building of at most 1.25 m; the whole footprint and 1 m of walking strip behind it
on one sidewalk island; clear of buildings, mapped poles, litter bins, hydrants, rings, post boxes, shelters, pay
stations, benches, TO360 signs, information pillars, Bike Share and each other; nothing is ever scattered where the City
has no record. Their colliders are solid like the benches' (a circle for a column, an oriented box for a corral) and are
in `allColliders`, so cafés, the walkers and the player keep clear. `explore.html` lists the new kinds to the CaféTO
layout as furniture, so the three prebuilt patio layouts were recorded again (`tools/build-patios.mjs`; the sites did not
change: 200 / 200 / 208).

`?mappedFurniture=0` leaves both new kinds out for an A/B timing; the default shows them.

## Checks

- `node --test tests/street-furniture.test.mjs`: snapshot metadata, the feed counts and the in-frame counts in both frames
  (litter pinned to the two geodata binaries), Existing-only and unique IDs, the generated files match the build, the
  placement rules on a synthetic street (sidewalk, roadway, obstruction, duplicate, orientation), the wiring in
  `js/street-props.js`, `explore.html` and `js/main.js`, and the two models' size, triangle budget, no emissive material
  and a single poster atlas.
- `tests/eglinton-frame.test.mjs` pins the new frame script as the eighth the Eglinton map swaps in.
- Captures on the Mac mini (`gpu2-local`, `tools/shots/mapped-street-furniture*.json`), both maps: Queen St W on the
  detail map and Eglinton Ave W on `?map=eglinton`, each in the real late-November season
  (`?liveClock=2026-11-26T17:00:00Z&liveDate=2026-11-26`, the trees bare; a preset would bring the summer trees back, so
  those shots use no preset), at night in November, and in summer (`liveDate=2026-07-15`).
- Frame time, six alternating runs of the main build and this one (`CAPTURE_UNCAPPED=1`, 1600 × 900, rAF mean over 6 s at
  a poster site and at the start): detail map 16.1–16.6 ms main, 16.6–16.8 ms this build (the display's 60 Hz); Eglinton
  map 23.4–23.7 ms main, 23.5–23.6 ms this build. The mini is shared, so this is relative evidence, not a budget.
