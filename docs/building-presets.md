# Building presets: every building's prototype, and the view that shows it

Bead `st-ndw9.10` (phase 1a) and `st-ndw9.11` of `docs/building-prototypes-spec.md`. Every building in the downtown
frame is given one of the catalogue's presets (`js/building-prototypes.js`), decided once on the build machine by
`tools/build-building-presets.mjs` and written to `data/toronto-building-presets.bin`, a small file the page fetches
only behind `?massing=1&presets=1`. A second file, `data/toronto-building-presets-debug.bin`, carries the facts the
debug view's identify panel shows and is fetched only behind `?presets=debug`. Nothing here changes how a building is
drawn in the normal view; drawing the presets is the next phase. Generated by
`node tools/build-building-presets.mjs --md docs/building-presets.md`; every number comes from its output.

## Inputs

- **`tools/data/building-inputs.json`** — the shared `buildings/3` facts per City 3D Massing part (form, house-scale,
  use class, area, heights, shop, address points), plus a **`neighbourhood`** column (the City neighbourhood number
  holding the part's centre, from `derived/neighbourhood-era/1/neighbourhoods.geojson`) and a **`yearBuilt`** column
  (the dataset's construction year where a source has one). 35,254 records.
- **`tools/data/downtown-era-draft-1.json`** — the hand-made split of house periods across the frame's neighbourhoods;
  a copy of the shared repo's `build/neighbourhood-era/downtown-era-draft-1.json`, byte for byte. It is an input that
  changes: John corrects neighbourhoods, the file is re-copied and the presets rebuilt.
- **`data/toronto-massing.bin`** — the sidecar, for the main-street flag on street edges, read through
  `js/massing-data.js` as the page does.
- **`js/buildings.js`** — its own shop-front rule (`storefrontSelection`, `signedWalls`, `buildingFront`,
  `shopFrontRule`), loaded here and called, so the assignment uses the same test the drawing does.
- **`js/building-prototypes.js`** — the catalogue (37 entries: 13 houses, the rest blocks, offices, civic, worship, industry, a `garage` and a `fallback`).

## The rules

| input | rule |
|---|---|
| main street | a shop front by Slop's rule (js/buildings.js) on a street-facing edge, 5 storeys or fewer, and not worship or civic: by era main-street-shop-early (Victorian & Edwardian) or main-street-shop-later (interwar on) |
| form | detached / semi / row -> a house preset by era (13); outbuilding -> garage; else the use-class table below |
| house era | the group's construction year where a source has one, else its neighbourhood's draft split drawn by the group's smallest durable id |
| apartments | 13+ on a podium glass-tower-podium; 13+ slab-tower; 6–12 mid-rise-block; else walk-up-apartment |
| business | 13+ dark-curtain-wall-tower; 6–12 contemporary-glass; else low-masonry-block |
| civic | a school name school; else 6+ storeys or 5,000 m²+ institutional-block; else small-civic |
| worship | religion, tradition, year and footprint (the section below): muslim mosque; jewish synagogue; buddhist, hindu or sikh temple; every other religion a church: before 1930 gothic when the tradition is roman catholic, anglican, presbyterian or orthodox or the footprint is 400 m² or more, else chapel; 1930 to 1959 chapel; 1960 on modern; undated: under 200 m² chapel, else by size |
| industrial | 2 storeys or fewer low-shed; else brick-warehouse |
| shed | low-shed |
| no use tag (none, or a residential tag on a non-house building) | 13+ tower; 6–12 mid-rise-block; else plain-low-block |
| other | fallback |

Where the table does not say how to choose between two presets, the simplest rule from the data is used: the storey
bands (`js/buildings.js` `FLOOR_HEIGHTS[0]` = 3.4 m), the sidecar's podium flag (`base` > 0), a school
name, and 5,000 m² for a civic block. Each is counted below.

(`js/buildings.js`: a source-keyed sign, a mapped retail place of its own, or a retail strip on the street it fronts),
is a shop front — whatever its form or use class, except worship and civic (and so schools), which keep their own
presets. The main-street rule runs ahead of the house and use-class rules. By era: **942**
`main-street-shop-early` (Victorian & Edwardian) and **280** `main-street-shop-later` (interwar
on), **1,222** in all. **941** taller buildings (over five storeys) with a

## The era

buildings), else its neighbourhood's draft split, drawn by the group's smallest durable id. Every part of a group
agrees, and the same building always gets the same era. A run of house buildings that share a wall is given **one**
era, from the run's smallest durable id: 17,773 runs hold 17,791 house buildings (median run
1, largest 3); 18 shared walls joined a run, and
0 were left alone because the two buildings are in different neighbourhoods (a
changed split must move only its own neighbourhood). Main-street buildings take their era the same way and agree in
their own runs (above).

## Places of worship (st-ndw9.16)

A **place of worship** is a building the shared `buildings/4` dataset gives a religion (or whose use class is
worship). Of **168** such buildings, **9** are a congregation in a house (every part
house-scale) and keep their house preset; the other **159** take one of six worship presets. The rule is
in `worshipPreset` (`tools/build-building-presets.mjs`), with its thresholds named in `WORSHIP`:

| input | rule |
|---|---|
| religion muslim | `mosque` |
| religion jewish | `synagogue` |
| religion buddhist, hindu or sikh | `temple` |
| every other religion (christian, or unnamed) | a church by year and size, below |
| a congregation in a house (every part house-scale) | its house preset (9 buildings) |

A church is dated by the group's own `yearBuilt` where a source has one (75 buildings), else by the
era its neighbourhood draws (84 buildings; the era-to-year map is `WORSHIP_ERA_YEAR`), else it has no
year (0 buildings, in a neighbourhood outside the draft's houses unsplit):

| dated year | preset |
|---|---|
| before 1930 | `church-gothic` when the tradition is roman catholic, anglican, presbyterian or orthodox **or** the footprint is 400 m² or more; else `church-chapel` |
| 1930 to 1959 | `church-chapel` |
| 1960 on | `church-modern` |
| no year, footprint under 200 m² | `church-chapel` (probably a rectory or a shop named for its congregation) |
| no year, footprint 200 m² or more | as a pre-1930 church: gothic when the tradition is high church or the footprint is 400 m² or more, else chapel |

The era-to-year map takes the era's own place on the worship scale, not the house era's: the house draw calls most of
the old city "Victorian" (before 1900), which would call almost every undated downtown church Gothic. Victorian and
Edwardian (on the map before 1920) read as **1900**, interwar as **1930**
(chapel), postwar as **1960** and recent as **2000** (modern).

## Limits

- **This phase does not draw the presets.** The normal view is unchanged; `?massing=1&presets=debug` paints each
  polygon building its preset's debug colour (a flat map of the assignment), and adds the legend and identify panel.
- **Era is a draft, not a measurement.** The neighbourhood split is the coordinator's judgement (draft 1); the
  per-building years cover 823 house buildings. The drawn split is a draw from the draft, not
  a count of real construction dates. The main-street era split uses the same draft.
- **The run rule is bounded to a neighbourhood and to a kind** so a changed split moves only its own neighbourhood; two
  wall-sharing buildings in different neighbourhoods, or a house beside a shop, may still differ.
- **The shop-front rule is the drawing's**, so it inherits its limits: a mapped retail place is one source, a retail
  strip needs two mapped places within 30 m, and a sign is only where one was surveyed.
- **Presets are form-and-era only.** They do not yet set roofs, facades, palettes or parts; the catalogue holds no
  drawing parameters until the next phase.

### Places of worship (st-ndw9.20)

The group's religion and tradition come from the debug file (`DEBUG.religion`, `tradition`, `names`). The group's largest part is the
nave, a compact part under 45% of its area and taller than it is the tower, the rest are wings. The City's height of a part far above a
nave of its width (4.5 m plus 0.45 of its width, 7 to 13 m, under a ridge of its pitch) is a spire or ridge, not an eave: the eave
takes the estimate. A Gothic church whose largest part is under 300 m2 takes the chapel's look; a larger one without a tower part gets
a tower at the end of its nave nearer the road. A mosque or temple whose nave is under 300 m2 or over 13 m tall is a converted building
and is marked with a small dome over the door (and a slim minaret); the others are a prayer hall of one or two tall storeys with a
large dome over its middle and a minaret at a corner from the ground. A synagogue has twin small towers and a round window at the
front of a one or two storey hall.

### House polish (st-ndw9.18)

Near-tier details, all flat-coloured triangles in the **near** roof batch (`pushRoof(A)` with `lod` false), so the far roof, the walls' mid
and far tiers and the phone tier never get them (`js/building-draw-detail.js`, `tests/building-draw.test.mjs`):

- **Sills and lintels** (Victorian and Edwardian houses: both; interwar: sills): one stone sill (4 triangles) and one lintel (2) under and over
  each front window. The window grid is the facade shader's own (`facadeCell`: corner piers, whole bays, the opening's share of the bay and
  of the storey), so they land on the shader's windows. Left out: behind a bay, in front of a vestibule, and on the ground floor of the door
  wall (the shader picks the door's bay, the plan does not know it).
- **Porch columns and steps** (Edwardian porches): the brick piers now stop at 0.95 m (plinths); a column above each carries the porch slab; the
  deck's edge and a low step the full width in front. The front stays open: the door's place is the shader's pick.
- **Bargeboards** also on the interwar vestibule gables (the era has them).
- **One back line per run** (`snapFronts`): the run's back wall is the upper median of its houses'; a deeper house is cut back to it (the rest is
  its rear wing), a shallower one is carried back by up to 2.5 m; a house further off keeps its own depth. The front line may now move up to
  2.6 m (was 1.5 m), and a front carried more than 1.5 m toward the street stays 5 m clear of the road's centreline. `tools/report-row-steps.mjs`
  counts the joined houses whose one roof (side gable, hip, mansard, flat) still steps.
- **Shop sign band**: over each 7.5 m storefront of a drawn shop, a fascia board (0.55 m, at the shader's fascia) in a colour of
  `js/storefront-signs.js` with a lighter name plate and no lettering (no name is invented). A real source-keyed sign stands in front of its stretch.
- **Modern church**: an A-frame (58 degrees), a steep gable or a steep shed, never a flat box; a slit of light glass up the street gable, a cross on the
  ridge, a slender tower with a cross. **Gothic tower**: a pointed door, slim lancets, a string course, a belfry of twin lancets, a cornice; a spire only
  where the data has the height (a tower part over four widths tall, or a nave far above its own height), else four pinnacles; a tower part the data
  has gets pointed heads over its windows. **Mosque and temple**: an entrance portal on the street end (a pointed arch under a small dome, a stepped stone
  frame, a red gate); a shikhara or a minaret on a prayer hall only where the data's height has room for it. Every mosque and temple the data has is a
  tall "converted" building with no compact tall part, so no new minaret or shikhara is drawn and the minaret a converted mosque had is left as it was.
- **Towers**: balcony slab tops are left out below the top three floors (seen only from above the floor, and behind the 1.05 m rail only from over 37
  degrees up), a parapet's 0.22 m top is left out, and a floor under a neighbour's roof gets no balcony band (`TRIM` in `js/building-draw-tall.js`).
